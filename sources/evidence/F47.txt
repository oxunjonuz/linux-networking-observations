Theme: what a network-base store costs in IPv6 -- there is no header checksum,
so the toll moves to the hop limit, and two offsets have no case at all.

No diff below: this measures the guard as it stands and proposes no change.

Linux 7.3.4, nftables in the initial user namespace, one packet at a time, one
rule at a time.  Six doors (local_out, postrouting, forward, prerouting, input
and the netdev egress hook of a veth), two classes (a datagram from a local
IPv6 socket, and a frame injected on the wire and forwarded), two base chains
per door (counter+log+accept, and the payload statement).  The receiver is an
IPv6 socket in its own netns plus that namespace's Ip6/Udp6 counters.
112 arms x 2 boots, identical on every derived fact.

1. The IPv6 network-base list has no case for offset 4 and none for 6.

  @nh,0,4  60 91 92 93  accepted (the written version nibble is 6)
  @nh,0,4  90 91 92 93  refused  -- case 0 compares the *immediate's* version
  @nh,2,2               accepted (flow label)
  @nh,4,2  payload_len  refused  -- no case for offset 4
  @nh,6,1  next header  refused  -- no case for offset 6
  @nh,8,16 saddr        accepted (the list allows len <= 32 here)
  @nh,24,16 daddr       accepted

The refusals are identical at all five layer-3 hooks and at the netdev egress
hook: unlike the ingress side, nothing here depends on the family the hook was
registered with.  A refused store is not a drop -- the packet continues and is
delivered byte-identical to the control.

2. With no header checksum the price of a header store mostly disappears.

One byte into @nh,7,1 (the hop limit), four values, seven door/class pairs:

  hop limit written   64 (as the frame carries it)  delivered everywhere
                      0x90                         delivered everywhere
                      0x00 / 0x01                  delivered at six pairs,
                                                   NO FRAME at prerouting

On IPv4 the same store killed the packet at four doors, because the header
checksum is computed before LOCAL_OUT and verified before PREROUTING.  IPv6 has
no such checksum, so the only thing that can charge for the store is the
forwarding path itself: `ip6_forward()` tests `hop_limit <= 1` *before* it
enters the FORWARD hook.  A store at PREROUTING is therefore seen by that test
and the packet dies in `ip6_forward()`; a store at the FORWARD hook happens
after the test and the frame is forwarded with a hop limit of zero.

The hop limit is decremented in the same gap, and the wire shows it: written at
prerouting, 0x40 leaves as 0x3f and 0x90 as 0x8f; written at forward,
postrouting or the egress hook, the value written is the value on the wire.
The read side sees the same seam -- reading @nh,7,1 and comparing with 0x40
matches at the doors the packet has not been forwarded through yet and matches
nowhere after it, because the byte there is 0x3f.

3. The transport toll is now the UDP pseudo-header, and it belongs to the skb.

One cell, @nh,8,16 = fd00::99, and its control (writing the address the frame
already carries):

  locally generated datagram   both delivered -- the skb leaves the hook with
                               CHECKSUM_PARTIAL and veth carries the flag to
                               the peer, which therefore never verifies it
  frame from the wire          the store costs the datagram: Udp6InErrors at
                               the receiver, no delivery; the control with the
                               same 16 bytes is delivered

Measured from the bytes, the wire field of a locally generated datagram is not
a valid checksum at all (fa2f where the pseudo-header gives 8220), and that is
true of the control arm as well -- it is a property of the sender, not of the
store.

4. A destination written before the route lookup stops being your datagram.

@nh,24,16 = fd00::99 at seven door/class pairs: the store is accepted
everywhere, and then

  local_out, postrouting   the packet is routed to the written address before
                           the route lookup, no neighbour exists, and the door
                           counts EIGHT packets for one datagram (the
                           solicitation retries and the ICMP errors of that
                           queue); nothing reaches the receiver
  prerouting               nothing arrives either
  netdev egress            the L2 header and the neighbour are already
                           resolved, so the frame goes out carrying the
                           written destination and the receiver counts
                           Ip6InAddrErrors

The counter of the door is not a property of the datagram: same rule, same
accepted store, 1 to 8 depending on the door.

Not tested here: IPv6 inside PPPoE, a VLAN-tagged IPv6 frame at an egress hook,
@th in this mode, extension headers at an egress hook, GSO/CHECKSUM_PARTIAL on
real hardware, ICMPv6 and SCTP as the transport.  Every rule here is installed
by root in the initial user namespace.

Reproduction: one static C program (netlink by hand), busybox initramfs, two
boots; every number above is derived from the log by an independent Python
model of the guard and of the door, and the tables are generated from the log
rather than typed.

# F47 — IPv6 at the egress doors: the toll moves from the header checksum to the hop limit, and it sits between two doors

Turn 569. Kernel 7.3.4-T563 (`1efbc95771acb424ec876dfa5c32ff1e993f5230b840c27ff00125978cbd2033`),
the image F41..F46 used; no new build. nftables 1.1.7, family `inet` chains installed by
root in the initial user namespace. Instrument `differ569` (mode `v6`), 112 arms × 2 boots,
and mode `iso` (the isolation of the F46 anomaly, section 9).

Six doors, two classes:

    door            family inet hook        class measured
    out             local_out               a datagram generated by a local IPv6 socket
    post            postrouting             local datagram / frame forwarded
    fwd             forward                 frame forwarded
    pre             prerouting              frame forwarded
    egr             netdev egress of x0     local datagram / frame forwarded

`loc` = a real `AF_INET6` socket bound to `fd00::1:40000` sending 18 bytes to
`fd00::2:5000`; the receiver is an `AF_INET6` socket on `fd00::2:5000` inside its own
netns, plus the kernel's `Ip6*`/`Udp6*` counters and an `AF_PACKET` tap on `x1`.
`fwd` = a fully built IPv6/UDP frame injected on `o1`, forwarded by the parent netns to
`fd00::2`. Both classes carry the same 18 bytes of `0x77`.

Two base chains per door (priority 0/10): the first is `counter`+`log`+`accept` and says
whether the hook ran, the second carries one `payload set` and says whether the guard
accepted it (`lives` = that chain's counter). Every logged message is kept, not only the
last, so the packets a door counts can be classified one by one.

## 1. The guard is the same at all six doors, and it has no case for two offsets

| cell (store) | out/loc | post/loc | egr/loc | post/fwd | fwd/fwd | egr/fwd | pre/fwd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hop90 | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| hop00 | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| hop01 | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| hop64 | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| flow2 | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| tc4 | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| ver9 | no | no | no | no | no | no | no |
| saddr | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| saddr_same | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| daddr | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| plen2 | no | no | no | no | no | no | no |
| nh1 | no | no | no | no | no | no | no |

Twelve cells, seven door/class pairs, both boots identical. `yes` = the store was accepted
(`lives > 0`), `no` = refused (`lives = 0`, and the counter of the door still 1: the refusal
is not a drop). Three cells are refused everywhere:

* `ver9` — `@nh,0,4 = 90 91 92 93`. `nft_nh_write_ok_ip6()` `case 0` compares the written
  **version nibble with 6**, so a store that would change the version is refused, and the
  same cell with `60 91 92 93` (`tc4`) is accepted;
* `plen2` — `@nh,4,2` (payload length) and `nh1` — `@nh,6,1` (next header): the IPv6 list
  has **no case for offset 4 and none for 6**, so these two fields are not writable at all
  at the network base. On IPv4 the same offsets are writable (F46 measured both).

The refusals are the same at five layer-3 hooks and at the netdev egress hook, which is the
opposite of F44's ingress result: nothing here depends on the family the hook was
registered with.

## 2. The coordinate is exact, and the read side agrees with it

`rd_src` and `rd_dst` (read the 16-byte source / destination and compare it with the
immediate) match at all seven pairs, and `rd_hop00` matches nowhere — the read counts:

| cell (read / refused) | out/loc | post/loc | egr/loc | post/fwd | fwd/fwd | egr/fwd | pre/fwd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ver9 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| plen2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| nh1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| rd_hop64 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| rd_hop00 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| rd_src | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| rd_dst | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

At the five layer-3 hooks the logged payload starts at the IPv6 header (`data = network
header`, `pkt->nhoff = 0`), so `@nh,off` is `data + off`; at the egress hook the logged
payload starts at the MAC header, so the store lands at `data + 14 + off`, and the tap shows
it in the right place. The two IPv6-specific traps of the read side (F40's `nhoff`, F45's
walk) do not appear here: `thoff` is not involved, and `nhoff` is zero on every door
measured.

## 3. The price: there is no header checksum, so six of seven doors are free

The same four one-byte stores into `@nh,7,1` (the hop limit), 7 pairs × 4 values:

On IPv4 the same store killed the packet at four doors, because the header checksum is
computed before LOCAL_OUT and verified before PREROUTING (F46 §2). IPv6 has no header
checksum, so:

* `hop64` (writing the value the frame already carries) is delivered everywhere — the store
  itself costs nothing;
* `hop90` is delivered everywhere, including the four doors where the IPv4 packet died;
* `hop00` and `hop01` — a hop limit of 0 or 1, which `ip6_forward()` refuses — are delivered
  at six of the seven pairs and produce **no frame at all** at `prerouting`. That is the
  whole toll of this family: `ip6_forward()` tests `hop_limit <= 1` *before* it enters the
  FORWARD hook, so a store at PREROUTING is seen by the test and a store at FORWARD is not.
  The route from "PREROUTING" to "FORWARD" is where the price is charged, and only there.

The decrement that pays for the forward sits in the same gap, and the wire shows it:

* written at `prerouting`, hop 0x40 leaves as `3f` and 0x90 leaves as `8f`;
* written at `forward`, `postrouting` or the egress hook, the value written is the value on
  the wire (the decrement already happened);
* the read side sees the same seam: `rd_hop64` (read the hop limit, compare with 0x40)
  matches at the five doors where the packet has not been forwarded yet — `out`, `post`,
  `egr` of the local class and `pre` of the forwarded one — and matches **nowhere** after
  it: at `forward`, `postrouting` and the egress hook of a forwarded packet the cell reads
  0x3f. A read at a door cannot see a value that the door's own packet no longer has.

The whole grid as the receiver saw it — the twelve store cells and the four read
cells, every door and class. `delivered` = the datagram reached the socket;
`Udp6InErr` = it reached the transport layer and died of the checksum;
`AddrErr` = the frame went out and the receiver counted `Ip6InAddrErrors`;
`no frame` = nothing arrived at all. The first arm of a boot has no baseline for
the receiver counters and reads `n/a`.

## 4. The transport toll moved into the pseudo-header

One cell, `@nh,8,16` (source address) = `fd00::99`, and its control `saddr_same` (the same
16 bytes the frame already carries):

| | local datagram (3 doors) | frame from the wire (4 doors) |
|---|---|---|
| `saddr` | delivered | **Udp6InErrors** at the receiver, no delivery |
| `saddr_same` | delivered | delivered |

The IPv6 header has no checksum, but the *UDP* checksum covers a pseudo-header that
contains the source address. So the toll that IPv4 charged for the header (F46: `InHdrErrors`
at four doors) is charged here for the transport, and again only where the checksum is
already final: a locally generated datagram leaves the hook with `CHECKSUM_PARTIAL`, veth
carries the flag to the peer, and the receiver never verifies it — measured directly from
the bytes, the UDPv6 checksum on the wire is not a valid one:

| door | field | sum of the datagram | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| out/loc | fa2f | d775 | invalid |
| post/loc | fa2f | d775 | invalid |
| egr/loc | fa2f | d775 | invalid |

That is a property of the sender, not of the store: the *control* datagram, with nothing
stored, has the same invalid field on the wire and is still delivered.

## 5. A destination written early stops being your datagram

`@nh,24,16` = `fd00::99`, one cell, seven pairs:

| door | cntp | lives | tapn | u6in_d | i6addr | pout_d |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| out/loc | 8 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 8 |
| post/loc | 8 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 8 |
| egr/loc | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| post/fwd | 7 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 6 |
| fwd/fwd | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| egr/fwd | 2 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 5 |
| pre/fwd | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 |

At `local_out` and at `postrouting` the store lands **before the route lookup**, so the
packet is routed to `fd00::99`, no neighbour exists, and the datagram is queued for a
resolution that never comes. The door counts **eight packets for one datagram** — the
solicitation retries and the ICMP errors of that queue — and nothing reaches the receiver.
At prerouting the same store also provokes traffic but nothing arrives. At the egress hook
the L2 header and the neighbour are already resolved, so the frame goes out *carrying the
written destination* (visible in the tap bytes) and the receiver counts `Ip6InAddrErrors`.

The counter of the door is therefore not a property of the datagram: same rule, same
accepted store, `cntp` between 1 and 8 depending on the door.

## 6. What was not measured

IPv6 inside PPPoE (`@nh` with `nhoff = 8` at an egress door); a VLAN-tagged IPv6 frame at
the egress hook; `@th` in this mode (F45 measured it on the ingress doors and F43/F44 on the
layer-3 ones); extension headers at an egress door (the walk happens before every hook, so
`@th` would land after them — read, not measured); GSO/`CHECKSUM_PARTIAL` on real hardware
(veth is what carries it here); ICMPv6 and SCTP as the transport; the `arp` family; more
than one device in a netdev chain; equal priorities.

Also not measured, and worth saying plainly: the *first* arm of each boot has no baseline
for the receiver counters, so its delivery column reads `n/a` in the tables above rather
than a number.

## 7. Errors of the turn, all mine

1. **The instrument's child had to yield before it drained.** Under TCG the frames wait in
   the backlog until the process that holds the CPU yields; the tap and the UDP socket were
   read before the softirq ran, so every arm looked undelivered while the counters said the
   packet had arrived. Found by reading the *disagreement* between `Ip6InReceives` and the
   tap, fixed with a 40 ms yield before the drain.
2. **The injected frame was addressed to the wrong device.** `ip6_rcv_core()` drops
   `PACKET_OTHERHOST` *before* it counts the packet, so a frame addressed to `o1` arriving on
   `o0` left no trace at all — not even in `Ip6InReceives`. The whole forwarded class was
   dead and looked like a guard refusal. Fixed by addressing the frame to `o0`.
3. **Two `/sys` oracles were dead**: `/sys/class/net/*/statistics/*_packets` read 0 in this
   image, so the columns derived from them were constants. They are printed but not used.
4. **The read arms compared with the wrong immediate** (`0x90` for a frame that carries
   `0x40`), so the first grid showed `lives = 0` for every read. The four read cells were
   rebuilt and are the `refused` table above.
5. **The receiver counters are cumulative and I first used them as if they were per-arm.**
   The verdict of an arm is the difference from the previous arm, and the first arm of a
   boot has no difference; both are handled explicitly now.
6. `pkill -f "tools/f47_run.sh full"` matched my own shell's command line and killed it —
   the same mistake as F39, for the same reason, in a command that was writing the text of
   that very pattern.
7. The first isolation build enabled IPv6 with `dad_transmits=1` written *before* the
   enable and got nothing: enabling recreates the device's conf tree. With the sysctls
   written after the enable, the kernel emits the MLD report and the DAD solicitation, which
   is what makes section 9 a measurement rather than a hope.

## 9. The F46 anomaly, closed

F46 recorded one arm where a single datagram was counted twice at the postrouting door and
wrote honestly that its counters could not separate "two rules in the chain" from "the hook
ran twice on one skb".

**From the frozen bytes of the F46 logs** (`tools/iso_f46_cntp.py`): on 856 arms
(428 × 2 boots) `cntp == npre` — the counter always equals the number of packets the chain's
own log emitted. Exactly one arm saw more than one packet: `rep1 post/loc n0_1`, `cntp = 2`,
`npre = 2`, and the payload the log kept is a **56-byte ICMPv6 router solicitation** from
`fe80::0c4f:63ff:fe58:4538` (the EUI-64 of `x0`'s MAC) to `ff02::2`, hop limit 255. The
receiver saw exactly one IPv4 datagram (`tapn = 1`, `IpInReceives +1`, `IpInDelivers +1`,
`udpn = 1`), and that arm carried no `F568 RETRY` — the two RETRY lines of that boot are at
`ll/out l0_2` and `th/out t6_2`, and both arms were re-installed clean.

**Reproduced on purpose** (mode `iso`, one `inet` base chain at postrouting, counter + log +
accept):

| arm | send4 | cnt | gcnt | nmsg | what |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ctl4 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 2 | v4/proto17,v4/proto1 |
| v6on | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | v6/nh0 |
| v6plus | 1 | 5 | 0 | 5 | v4/proto17,v4/proto1,v6/nh0,v6/nh0,v6/icmp6-135 |
| guard | 1 | 4 | 2 | 4 | v4/proto17,v4/proto1,v6/nh0,v6/nh0 |
| v6off | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | v4/proto17,v4/proto1 |
| quiet | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | - |

* `ctl4` — IPv6 off, one IPv4 datagram: the chain counts **2**, the datagram and the ICMP
  port-unreachable it provokes. Both IPv4.
* `v6on` — IPv6 enabled on `x0` and **not a byte sent by the rule**: the chain counts **1**,
  and the payload is IPv6 (next header 0: a hop-by-hop header carrying an MLD report).
  This is the anomaly in one line: an `inet` counter counts packets nobody sent.
* `v6plus` — the same enable plus one IPv4 datagram in one arm: **5** — one IPv4 datagram,
  one ICMPv4 error, two IPv6 hop-by-hop packets and one ICMPv6 neighbour solicitation.
* `guard` — the same traffic with a `meta nfproto ipv4` rule in front of a second counter:
  the blind counter sees **4**, the family-specific one sees **2**. The same packets, one
  counter that can tell the families apart.
* `v6off` — IPv6 off again: **2**, exactly the IPv4-only baseline, and the family-specific
  counter agrees.
* `quiet` ×4 — nothing sent, nothing counted.

So `cntp = 2` in F46 was never a double count: an `inet` chain counts every family, the
second packet was IPv6, and it was emitted by the address configuration of the sending
device, not by the rule. The debt F46 left open is closed by a counter that can be driven to
zero and a guard that separates the families, not by re-reading the log.

Not a vulnerability: every rule here is installed by root in the initial user namespace.
