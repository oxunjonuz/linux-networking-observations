F74 -- what the offset charge does to the queue, and where the head of a copied
skb comes from

Kernel 7.3.4-T563 (`1efbc957…2033`), no new build. Same three-netns IPv4 path
as F65-F73. Thirty arms over two boots, two attempts each, every modelled field
identical between the boots and between attempts within a boot.

1. The receive queue holds `floor(sk_rcvbuf / charge)` datagrams, and the
   boundary is a byte of the socket's own request. 64 zerocopy datagrams of
   512 bytes, `SO_RCVBUF` doubled by the kernel into `sk_rcvbuf`:

       R = 38400   charge 5056  ->  7 kept, rmem 35392
       R = 51200   charge 5056  -> 10 kept, rmem 50560
       R = 76800   charge 5056  -> 15 kept, rmem 75840
       R = 102400  charge 5056  -> 20 kept, rmem 101120
       R = 38400   charge 9152  ->  4 kept, rmem 36608
       R = 51200   charge 9152  ->  5 kept, rmem 45760
       R = 76800   charge 9152  ->  8 kept, rmem 73216
       R = 102400  charge 9152  -> 11 kept, rmem 100672

   Two arms pin the boundary: `SO_RCVBUF` 17695 (R 35390) keeps 6, and 17696
   (R 35392 = 7 x 5056) keeps 7 -- one byte of the request, one datagram. Two
   more leave `rmem` equal to `sk_rcvbuf` to the byte and keep all 64
   (323584 = 64 x 5056 and 585728 = 64 x 9152), which only works because the
   check in `__udp_enqueue_schedule_skb()` is `rmem + size > rcvbuf`, strictly.

2. The same 64 datagrams are 7 or 4 depending on a byte the receiver cannot
   see. 512 bytes at page offset 0 cost 5056; at offset 3600, 9152; a
   4097-byte payload at offset 0 also costs 9152. The gate record is identical
   in all three -- `len` 512, `dlen` 512, `end` 384, `tail` 44, no clone -- so
   the socket cannot tell them apart, and the number of datagrams it will hold
   before dropping (and therefore where it starts dropping) is decided by the
   sender's buffer alignment. The forward rule's own shape changes nothing: a
   counter-only chain prints the same 7 / 35392 / 57 as one that logs.

3. Above half the buffer the charge can disappear, and that is the receiver's
   own branch. `skb_condense()` pulls a payload into the head when
   `data_len <= end - tail` (340 bytes for a zerocopy skb) and charges
   `SKB_TRUESIZE(end)` = 960. 64 datagrams of 200 bytes at `R` = 38400 keep
   18 with 33664 in the queue -- four at 5056 and fourteen at 960 -- where the
   same datagrams of 512 bytes keep 7. So the page charge a misaligned sender
   inflicts is bounded by whether the payload fits the head's tailroom, and the
   same branch can cut the bill by 57344 bytes.

4. Where a copied skb's `end` comes from: the small-head cache, and the
   boundary is a byte of payload. `kmalloc_reserve()` gives every skb whose
   `SKB_HEAD_ALIGN(size)` is at most `SKB_SMALL_HEAD_CACHE_SIZE` a head of
   exactly that size -- 704 in this kernel, read from the booting kernel's own
   `skbuff_small_head` slab cache -- and `end` is that size minus
   `SKB_DATA_ALIGN(sizeof(struct skb_shared_info))` = 320, so `end` = 384 and
   `truesize` = 960 for **every** copy with a payload up to 325 bytes. One byte
   more (326) and the request `payload + 59` leaves the small head, the
   allocator rounds up to 1024 (there is no 768 class), and `end` jumps to 704
   and the bill to 1280. Measured on the wire for 270, 277, 278, 309, 310 and
   325 (384/960) against 326 and 342 (704/1280) and 5000 (7872/8448).

5. A queue with a mixed charge sequence is not `floor(R / mean)`: the check runs
   for every datagram, so a charge that was refused does not stop a later,
   smaller one. With charges alternating 9152 and 5056 at `R` = 51200 the
   seventh (9152) is refused at 51776 and the eighth (5056) is accepted,
   47680, 7 datagrams where `floor(51200/9152)` = 5 would suggest. The order
   matters as much as the multiset: the same charges starting with 5056 keep 5
   and bill 33472 instead of 37568.

Two of my predictions were refuted in this turn by my own arithmetic (a
simulation that stopped at the first refusal, and a sender whose 2048-byte
stride mixed two offsets into one arm); both are recorded, with the numbers
they got wrong, in the finding. Nothing here is a memory-safety claim: every
rule was installed by root in the initial user namespace, and the effect is
accounting -- the sender's iov alignment, and the receiver's own condense
branch, both move the number of datagrams a socket will hold for a given
`sk_rcvbuf`.

# F74 — the receiver's queue capacity is set by a byte in the sender's iov, and the head of a copy is the small-head cache floor

Turn 596. Kernel `7.3.4-T563` (`1efbc957…2033`), **no new build**. Instrument
`differ596`, modes `q` (21 arms) and `h` (9 arms) — **30 arms × 2 attempts ×
2 boots = 120 measured attempts**. Predictions frozen before the first boot
(`research/predictions_596.md`, `743c6360…6f5e`) and, after one smoke that
found a flaw in my own instrument, a second frozen file
(`research/predictions_596b.md`, `878121a5…3b68`) before the counted boots.
Neither file was edited afterwards. Five predictions of the first file and two
of the second were refuted; all seven refutations are in §6.

## 0. The two debts

F73 §"Что не измерено, прямо", first bullet:

> почему у 5000-байтной линейной копии `end` = 7872, а у 512-байтной 704:
> тождество `ts = end + 576` держится для обеих, но откуда берётся `end`
> (корзина аллокатора, а не полезная нагрузка) — прочитано, а не измерено.

and the plan named at the end of turn 595:

> следствие заряда для очереди: 64 zerocopy-датаграммы из выровненного буфера
> против тех же 64 со смещением 3600 при `sk_rcvbuf` 38400 (ожидание 7 против 4).

Both are answered by cells here. §1–§4 answer the second debt's first half, §5
the second.

## 1. The law: a queue accepts while the bill fits, and the check is `>`

`__udp_enqueue_schedule_skb()` (`net/ipv4/udp.c:1678`) reads the bill of the
skb **before** anything shrinks it, drops when `rmem + size > rcvbuf`, accepts
a datagram into an empty queue regardless, and runs `skb_condense()` only when
the queue is already above half. For one constant charge `s` that is
`kept = floor(R/s)` with `R = sk_rcvbuf = 2 × SO_RCVBUF`. Eight arms with a
constant charge (`ts` identical on all 64 gate records of the arm) and eight
buffers:

| arm | R | charge s | kept | rmem | errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q64_a0_R38`     | 38400  | 5056 | **7**  | 35392  | 57 |
| `q64_a0_R50`     | 51200  | 5056 | **10** | 50560  | 54 |
| `q64_a0_R76`     | 76800  | 5056 | **15** | 75840  | 49 |
| `q64_a0_R102`    | 102400 | 5056 | **20** | 101120 | 44 |
| `q64_4096a36_R38`  | 38400  | 9152 | **4**  | 36608  | 60 |
| `q64_4096a36_R50`  | 51200  | 9152 | **5**  | 45760  | 59 |
| `q64_4096a36_R76`  | 76800  | 9152 | **8**  | 73216  | 56 |
| `q64_4096a36_R102` | 102400 | 9152 | **11** | 100672 | 53 |

Two cells pin the boundary from both sides, and each is a byte of the
*socket's* request:

| arm | SO_RCVBUF | R | kept | rmem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q64_a0_one` | 17695 | 35390 | **6** | 30336 |
| `q64_a0_eq`  | 17696 | **35392** = 7·5056 | **7** | **35392** |
| `q64_a0_full` | 161792 | **323584** = 64·5056 | **64** | **323584** |
| `q64_4096a36_full` | 292864 | **585728** = 64·9152 | **64** | **585728** |

`q64_a0_one` and `q64_a0_eq` differ by **one byte of the request** and by one
datagram kept; the two `_full` arms leave `rmem` equal to `sk_rcvbuf` to the
byte and keep all 64, which is only possible because the check is strictly
greater. Errors and kept add up to 64 in every arm of the table above.

The forward rule does not touch the bill: `q64_a0_cn`, a chain that only
counts and never logs, prints the same 7 / 35392 / 57 as `q64_a0_R38`.

## 2. The same 64 datagrams, 7 or 4: a byte the receiver cannot see

One sender, one socket, one door, 64 datagrams of 512 bytes, `sk_rcvbuf`
38400 — and the only difference is where the payload starts in its page:

| arm | page offset of datagram 0 | gate `ts` | kept | rmem | errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q64_a0_R38`  | 0    | 5056 | **7** | 35392 | 57 |
| `q64_a36_R38` | 3600 | **9152, 5056, 9152, 5056, …** | **5** | 37568 | 59 |
| `q64_4096a36_R38` | 3600 | 9152 | **4** | 36608 | 60 |
| `q64_4097a0`  | 0 (payload 4097 crosses a page) | 9152 | **4** | 36608 | 60 |

The receiver's socket cannot see which of these a datagram is: `len` = 512,
`dlen` = 512, `end` = 384, `tail` = 44, `fl` = 0 on every record of every arm.
The queue keeps 7 datagrams (3584 payload bytes) at offset 0 and 4 (2048
bytes) when the sender's iov starts at 3600 or when the payload itself crosses
a page — for 35392 against 36608 bytes of memory. **The number of datagrams a
receiver can hold is decided by the sender's buffer alignment**, and so is the
point at which it starts dropping.

## 3. The mixed sequence, and two predictions my arithmetic got wrong

The first smoke found that my own sender advances its payload pointer by
`F591_MAXPL` = 2048 (`vm/differ595_add.c`), so an `al=2` burst does not sit at
one offset — it alternates 3600 and 1552, and therefore 9152 and 5056. Those
arms were kept, because a mixed sequence tests the algorithm harder. Measured,
both attempts, both boots:

| arm | R | kept | rmem | errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q64_a36_R38`  | 38400  | **5**  | **37568** | 59 |
| `q64_a36_R50`  | 51200  | **7**  | **47680** | 57 |
| `q64_a36_R76`  | 76800  | **11** | **76096** | 53 |
| `q64_a36_R102` | 102400 | **14** | **99456** | 50 |
| `q64_a36_full` | 585728 | **64** | **454656** | 0 |

The lesson of the row `R` = 51200: **a dropped 9152 does not stop a later
5056.** The queue is checked again for every datagram: the seventh charge
(9152) fails at 42624 + 9152 = **51776 > 51200**, and the eighth (5056) still
fits, 42624 + 5056 = **47680**, so the queue ends with 7 datagrams — more
datagrams and less memory than `floor(R/9152)` = 5 would suggest. The phase matters as much as the
multiset: `q64_4096a0_R38`, the same charges as `q64_a36_R38` but starting
with 5056, keeps the same 5 datagrams and bills **33472** instead of 37568.

## 4. Above half a buffer the page charge disappears — for small payloads

`skb_condense()` pulls the payload into the head when `data_len` fits the
head's free room, which for a zerocopy skb is `end − tail` = 384 − 44 = 340
bytes, and then charges `SKB_TRUESIZE(end)` = 960. 64 datagrams of **200**
bytes at offset 0, `sk_rcvbuf` 38400 (`q64_z200`):

| number | measured | arithmetic |
|---|---|---|
| gate `ts` | 5056 on all 64 records | the probe reads the skb before condense |
| kept | **18** | 4 · 5056 = 20224, then 14 · 960 |
| rmem | **33664** | 20224 + 13440 |
| errors | 46 | 64 − 18 |

The same 64 datagrams of 512 bytes keep 7; the 200-byte ones keep 18, because
from the fifth on the queue is above half (`R >> 1` = 19200) and the receiver's
own `skb_condense()` erases the sender's page charge. The branch that exists to
save memory saves **57344** bytes here: 18 · 5056 = **91008** would have been
the bill without it, and the bill is 33664 — the receiver's accounting, not the
sender's buffer, is what moves.

## 5. Where `end` comes from: the small-head cache, and a boundary that is a byte

`kmalloc_reserve()` (`net/core/skbuff.c:605`) gives every skb whose
`SKB_HEAD_ALIGN(size)` is at most `SKB_SMALL_HEAD_CACHE_SIZE` a head of exactly
that size (`:618`), and `__finalize_skb_around()` sets `end` to that size minus
`SKB_DATA_ALIGN(sizeof(struct skb_shared_info))`. The booting kernel's own
slab cache says the number: `F596 SLAB|src=sysfs|obj=704`. Nine copied
datagrams, one per arm:

| payload | `end` at the wire | `ts` | the arm's identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 270 | **384** | 960 | `ts = end + 576` |
| 277 | **384** | 960 | |
| 278 | **384** | 960 | |
| 309 | **384** | 960 | |
| 310 | **384** | 960 | |
| **325** | **384** | **960** | the last payload in the small head |
| **326** | **704** | **1280** | one byte more, one kmalloc class up |
| 342 | **704** | 1280 | |
| 5000 | **7872** | 8448 | `kmalloc_size_roundup(5440)` = 8192 |

So `end` is not a property of the payload at all: it is `ksize − 320`, where
`ksize` is 704 for everything up to a 325-byte payload and the allocator's
class above it — and the class list the boot printed has no 768, which is why
the step is 320 and not 64. The request is `payload + 59`: `28` bytes of IP
and UDP headers, `hh_len` = 16 (`LL_RESERVED_SPACE` of a veth with a zero
`needed_headroom`: `((14 + 0) & ~15) + 16`) and `15` of tailroom
(`net/ipv4/ip_output.c:1108-1136`). Three hypotheses were on the table before
the run — boundary at 277/278 if the alignment unit were 64, at 309/310 if
`needed_headroom` were 14, at 325/326 if it is 0 and the unit is 16. The step
is between 325 and 326, so `hh_len` = 16 and the unit is 16, and the plateau
below is flat at 960 across five payloads.

## 6. Predictions: which held, which did not

Held: of the first file **P1, P3, P5, P7, P9, P10, P11, P13, P14, P15** (mode
`q`) and **P16–P24** (mode `h`, all nine, including the step at 326 and the
5000-byte bucket); of the second file **P26–P30, P33, P34, P35**.

Refuted, all seven, and each with its cause:

| P | said | measured | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| P2  | 4 / 36608 | **5 / 37568** | my sender's 2048-byte stride: `al=2` alternates 9152 and 5056 (§3) |
| P4  | 5 / 45760 | **7 / 47680** | same stride |
| P6  | 8 / 73216 | **11 / 76096** | same stride |
| P8  | 11 / 100672 | **14 / 99456** | same stride |
| P12 | 64 / 585728 | **64 / 454656** | same stride: the 64 charges are 32 × 9152 + 32 × 5056 |
| P31 | 6 / 42624 | **7 / 47680** | my simulation stopped at the first drop (§3) |
| P32 | 10 / 71040 | **11 / 76096** | same |

P2–P12 are the same error five times: I predicted the curve of a constant
offset for arms that deliver two offsets. The numbers those five predictions
intended are carried by `q64_4096a36_*`, which really do carry one charge
(P26–P30) — measured after the flaw was found and before the counted boots.
P31/P32 are a second, separate error of mine: in a mixed sequence the queue is
re-examined for every datagram, so it does not stop at the first refusal; the
arm `q64_a36_R102` matched by luck only because after 99456 nothing else fit.

## 7. Ошибки хода, все мои

1. **The sender's stride** (§3): five refuted predictions from one unread line
   of my own instrument. Found by the first smoke, not by thinking.
2. **My simulation stopped at the first drop** (§3): P31/P32 wrong in a way
   that would have hidden the most interesting sentence of §3.
3. **The trace was never cleared**: F73's own error #2 (`echo > trace`, not
   `echo >> trace`) came back the moment a boot ran 21 arms of 64 datagrams:
   by arm 13 the 2 MB read window no longer reached the markers, `mark=0`, and
   every counter read zero while the receiver still reported the right queue.
   The first counted attempt was killed and the campaign restarted; the fix is
   `O_TRUNC` before every arm (`f596_clear()`), which is what the shell's
   `echo > trace` does and what `f595_wf` (open `O_WRONLY`) never did.
4. **The verifier's post-condense charge was 576, not 960** — I wrote
   `SKB_DATA_ALIGN_SHINFO + SKB_DATA_ALIGN_SKB` where the head's own 384 bytes
   belong. It said the 200-byte arm should keep 27 and bill 33472; the log says
   18 and 33664. Found by running the verifier, not by reading it.
5. **The same 576 in the charge base** of every arm's sequence, which made the
   first verifier run disagree with every gate record.
6. **The reader's buffer is 4096 bytes**: a 4097-byte datagram is *read* as
   4096. The verifier now expects `min(payload, 4096)` and says why; the
   measured `dl0` of that arm is the witness's limit, not the kernel's.
7. **The verifier crashed instead of going red** when an RCV line was deleted
   (a `None` in the InErrors subtraction) — found by the selftest, fixed with a
   presence check.
8. **Two source anchors were wrong** (a rule wrapped over two lines, and a
   stray closing parenthesis in my needle); the search is now
   whitespace-insensitive.
9. The arm table's `exp_charge` labels on the mixed arms still carry the
   first-file numbers. They are labels printed by the instrument, not checks:
   the verifier compares the printed table against the source and computes its
   own model, and no check reads `exp_charge`.

## 8. Что не измерено, прямо

- The mixed-sequence arms' charges are *predicted* by the 2048-byte stride and
  match the first 16 gate records; the last 48 of each arm are not printed
  (the instrument caps its arrays at 16), so the sequence beyond the 16th is
  inferred, not read.
- No arm brings the queue to exactly `rcvbuf >> 1` at a condense decision, so
  the strictness of *that* comparison is not pinned here (F70 pinned it with
  its own arms; a mutation `>` → `>=` survives this turn's arms and is named in
  `tools/verify596_selftest.py`).
- No arm sends a datagram larger than the whole buffer, so the empty-queue
  clause and the second (locked) `udp_rmem_schedule()` check are never the
  reason for an outcome here — both survive mutation and are named.
- The 200-byte arm's `skb_condense()` call is inferred from the arithmetic
  (4 × 5056 + 14 × 960), not from a probe on the function: this instrument has
  no probe there (F71/F73 measured it directly).
- IPv6, TCP, tunnels, `CHECKSUM_COMPLETE`, a real NIC, `SO_RCVBUFFORCE`: not
  touched.
- **Не уязвимость**: every rule was installed by root in the initial user
  namespace (F57), and the effect is accounting — a sender's iov alignment
  changes the receiver's bill per datagram by up to one page. It is not memory
  corruption, and the same branch that raises the bill (§4) can also cut it.
