F65 -- the packet that nothing follows

A UDP datagram that begins a GRO flow and is not followed by another one is
not delivered when it is sent.  On 7.3.4-T563 with x0's gro_flush_timeout at
200 ms it reaches the forward chain 201 ms later, and a datagram that starts a
new flow inside the poll that the timer itself runs reaches it one flush period
after that: 402 ms, against 201 ms for the aggregate that preceded it in the
same send.  The rule sees one send as two packets: an aggregate whose uh->len
is the sum of three datagrams (0x04d0, a length no datagram in it has, with the
first datagram's own CHECKSUM_PARTIAL seed 0xfcc2 in the checksum field) and a
plain packet carrying the sender's untouched header (0x0208, 0xfcc2).

A two-byte store into the UDP length field then has three fates, decided by the
shape and not by the door: on a fraglist through a door that segments in
software the splitter puts the head's length back (udp_set_len_short(8+mss)) and
every datagram is delivered (512, 512, 200, 24); on a fraglist through a door
that keeps GSO, and on a linear aggregate, every segment's checksum is computed
against the stored number and the aggregate dies whole (3 errors, only the
24-byte leftover delivered).

The byte cap that ends aggregation is 65536 and it counts one byte more than the
payload: 64 datagrams merge up to payload 1022 (64 x 1023 < 65536) and at 1023
the 64th is refused (63 + 1).  UDP_GRO_CNT_MAX = 64 binds only below payload
1023; above it the byte cap binds first, and at payload 1200 the aggregate is
54 + 10.  The zero-length branch of udp_set_len() at GRO_LEGACY_MAX_SIZE is
therefore unreachable for UDP on this kernel: both merge paths refuse at
65536, so the largest UDP length a rule can read here is 0xff88 (65416).

STATUS: NOT SENT. Follow-up to the F28 report; no patch is proposed.

# F65 — the packet that nothing follows: one send, two packets, two headers,
# and a byte cap that counts one byte more than the payload

Kernel `7.3.4-T563` (`1efbc957…2033`), the same image as F41..F64; nothing rebuilt.
Instrument `vm/differ587_add.c` (generated `vm/differ587.c` from `differ586.c`),
modes `flow` (9 arms) and `cap` (16 arms), **two boots × two attempts per arm**,
three netns, F64's topology unchanged: `S` sends, the parent is the router
(`x0` in, `y1`/`y2` out, one rule per arm in `ip6` table `f85p` chain `fwd`),
`D` receives with a plain socket on 5000 and a `UDP_GRO` socket on 5001, a
witness chain in its own netns, taps on `y3`/`y4`.

Predicted before the first run: `research/predictions_587.md`
(`6473a89f178f32225d2468aee27a5e7c80c1af133cfcf387f454b77089c8e5a2`).

**What is new in the instrument.** F64's reader gave up 42 ms after it started,
and the reason it missed the packet that nothing follows is exactly that. This
receiver polls both sockets every 2 ms for **700 ms** and stamps every datagram
it gets, so "when" is a number and not a guess. (The nft counter is no help: one
`GETRULE` dump costs ~100 ms, which cannot time a 200 ms timer.)

## 1. The datagram that nothing follows waits for the flush timer — and then one tick more

| arm | datagrams | what the rule counts | packets the rule sees | when the socket gets them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `l_one_a` | 1×512 | **1** | 560 bytes, `uh->len` `0x0208` | **201 ms** |
| `l_one_b` | 1×512 (`y2`) | 1 | 560, `0x0208` | 201 ms |
| `l_two_a` | 2×512 | 1 | 1072, `0x0408` | 200–202 ms |
| `l_two_u` | 2×512 (linear) | 1 | 1072, `0x0408`, check **seed(1032)** | 200 ms |
| `l_mid_a` | 512,512,200,512 | **2** | 1272 (`0x04d0`) + 560 (`0x0208`) | **201, 201, 201, then 402** |
| `l_mid_b` | the same (`y2`) | 2 | the same two | 200, 200, 200, then 400 |

Two mechanisms, both measured:

* **the first packet of a send is late by the deferred NAPI.** `x0` has
  `gro_flush_timeout` 200 ms and `napi_defer_hard_irqs` 1 (F63's setting), so
  the poll that would deliver the completed aggregate runs only when the timer
  fires: every arm's first datagram arrives at **186–207 ms**, never at 0.
* **the leftover is one flush period behind the aggregate it did not join.**
  `l_mid_a`: the aggregate of three is completed by the *mismatch* (the 200-byte
  datagram ends the flow), but it is delivered in the poll that the timer
  schedules, and the fourth datagram — added to the fresh flow list in that same
  poll — is kept by `__gro_flush_chain(flush_old = true)` because its
  `age == jiffies` (`net/core/gro.c`), so it leaves on the **next** tick:
  402 ms. In an arm where a second poll runs with `work_done = 0`
  (`gro_flush_normal(gro, false)`) the same leftover leaves at once — the
  difference between 205 ms (`l_mid_len_a`) and 402 ms (`l_mid_a`) is that
  branch, and both were measured.

F64's `g_mid_a` missed this datagram because its reader stopped 42 ms after it
started; the datagram was not lost, it was 200 ms behind.

## 2. One send, two packets, two headers — and the rule cannot tell which is which

`l_mid_a`, one socket, four `sendto` calls, one rule instance:

| packet | IPv6 payload length | `uh->len` | `uh->check` |
|---|---|---|---|
| the aggregate of three | `0x04d0` (1232 = 8+512+512+200) | `0x04d0` | `fcc2` = **seed(520)** — the first datagram's own seed |
| the leftover | `0x0208` (520) | `0x0208` | `fcc2` = seed(520) |

The aggregate's length is a number **no datagram in it has**; its checksum
field is the *head datagram's* seed on the fraglist path and seed(whole) on the
linear one (`l_two_u`: `uh->check` = seed(1032)). Which of the two the rule
reads is decided by a device bit (`rx-gro-list` vs `rx-udp-gro-forwarding`) that
the rule cannot see — F63's finding, now measured on a two-datagram send.

## 3. A store into the length field: the shape decides, not the door

`@th,4,2 = 0020` on the mid profile. The rule writes both packets — the
aggregate (field `0x0020`, skb still 1232 bytes) and the leftover (`0x0020`,
skb 520 bytes) — and then three different fates follow:

| arm | shape / door | delivered | `Udp6InErrors` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `l_mid_len_a` | fraglist, `y1` (GSO kept) | **1**: the 24-byte leftover | **+3** |
| `l_mid_len_b` | fraglist, `y2` (software split) | **4**: 512, 512, 200, **24** | 0 |
| `l_mid_len_u` | linear, `y2` | **1**: the 24-byte leftover | **+3** |

P5 predicted that the splitter "erases" the store on the aggregate and all
three datagrams are delivered. **Refuted**: only the fraglist path through a
door that segments in software erases it — `__udp_gso_segment_list()` puts the
head's length back with `udp_set_len_short(uh, 8+mss)` and `skb_segment_list()`
copies nothing. The other two shapes compute each segment's checksum against
the *stored* number (`csum16_sub(uh->check, uh->len)`), so every segment is
wrong and the aggregate dies whole. The 24-byte datagram is the same cell F64
measured for a lone packet; here it arrives **after** the aggregate's three
deaths, in the same send, under the same rule.

## 4. The byte cap: 65536 counts one byte more than the payload — boundary at 1023/1024

The count cap `UDP_GRO_CNT_MAX = 64` is not what binds for long datagrams.
Measured (one aggregate per arm, message length = 40 + `uh->len`):

| arm | payload | merged | `uh->len` | the rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `k512x64_a` | 512 | 64 | `0x8008` (8+32768) | — |
| `k961x64_a` | 961 | 64 | `0xf048` (8+61504) | — |
| `k1016x64_a` | 1016 | 64 | `0xfe08` (8+65024) | — |
| `k1022x64_a` | 1022 | **64** | `0xff88` (8+65408) | — |
| `k1023x64_a` | 1023 | **63** | `0xfbc9` (8+64449) | own packet, `uh->len` `0x0407` |
| `k1024x64_a` | 1024 | 63 | `0xfc08` | own packet, `0x0408` |
| `k1200x64_a` | 1200 | **54** | `0xfd28` (8+64800) | second aggregate of 10, `0x2ee8` |
| `k961x65_a` | 961 ×65 | 64 | `0xf048` | the 65th alone, `0x03c9` |

The bisection is exact: 64 datagrams merge while `64 × (payload + 1) < 65536`,
i.e. up to payload **1022**; at **1023** the 64th is refused. The cap is
`65536` and it counts **one byte more than the payload per datagram**
(`skb_gro_receive_list()`: `p->len + skb->len >= 65536`). My prediction said the
boundary was at 961/962 (I counted the whole skb, 62+payload); that is
**refuted**, and the six bisection arms were added *after* the first run — they
test a derivation from source (`skb_pull(skb, skb_gro_offset(skb))` leaves the
payload), and the derivation is wrong by one byte. What the byte is, is a named
debt.

`k961x65_a` separates the two caps: the same two packets as `k1023x64_a`, but
for the opposite reason — 64 fit by bytes, and the 65th is refused by the
**count**.

## 5. BIG TCP's zero length is unreachable for UDP on this kernel

No arm shows `uh->len == 0` or an IPv6 payload length of 0; the largest message
measured is 65 520 bytes (`k1022x64_a`). Both merge paths refuse at
`>= 65536`: `skb_gro_receive()` for any non-TCP flow
(`if (unlikely(p->len + len >= GRO_LEGACY_MAX_SIZE)) { if (proto != IPPROTO_TCP
|| encapsulated) return -E2BIG; }`) and `skb_gro_receive_list()`
unconditionally. So `udp_set_len()`'s
`len < GRO_LEGACY_MAX_SIZE ? htons(len) : 0` always takes the first branch and
`udp_get_len()`'s `skb_is_gso` branch is dead for UDP GRO here. That is an
answer about **reachability**, not a bug — and it closes the debt F64 named.

## 6. The delivery split is a race, and it is not a checksum failure

For arms where 64 datagrams arrive as one GSO skb on `y1` (the receiver splits
them itself) **and** on `y2`, the socket received between **29 and 64** of them
and `Udp6InErrors` rose by the difference: `k1200x64_a` 54+10, `k1200x64_b`
54+10 (56+8 in the other boot), `k1022x64_a` 29+35, `k512x64_a` **64+0**. The
conservation `delivered + errors == datagrams` holds in all 100 measured
attempts, and the delivered ones start at index 0; the *size* of the prefix
varies between two attempts of the same arm and between boots. It is not a
checksum error (that would be deterministic): the candidate is the receive
queue's memory limit charged segment by segment while a burst is enqueued
faster than the reader drains it. **Named debt, not a conclusion.**

## Predictions: 6 held, 5 refuted or narrowed

Held: P1 (the lone packet is the sender's own header), P2 (fraglist seed vs
linear seed), P3 (the mismatch completes the aggregate at once, the leftover
waits), P4 (the leftover is late by the timer — 402 vs 201 ms, and the
`flush_old = false` branch at 205 ms), P7/P8 (the cap arithmetic of
`k961x65_a` and `k1200x64_a`), P12 (every `sendto` returns its length).

Refuted or narrowed: **P5** (the store is erased only on the fraglist+software
split; the other two shapes kill the aggregate), **P6** (the byte boundary is
1023/1024, not 961/962), **P9** (BIG TCP is unreachable — held — but the
largest measured length is 65 520, not 61 512), **P10** (door `b` does *not*
always deliver everything: 54+10 in one boot, 56+8 in the other), **P11**
(`Udp6InDatagrams` equals the datagram count only when nothing is dropped; for
bursts it equals the delivered prefix).

## Errors of the turn, all mine

1. **F64's parent taps never opened** — the globals `F585_TA`/`F585_TB` start at
   fd 0 and `f586_taps()` only opens a tap whose fd is negative, so every
   `an`/`anall` in F64 was a dead witness. Found by this verifier; the fix
   (`fd = -1`) is in `f587_setup()`, and even with it the parent taps stayed
   empty in this build — all frame bytes below come from the *receiver's* tap.
2. **The counter trace was useless**: `f555_count()` costs ~100 ms per call, so
   a 25 ms sampling loop measured its own latency (the first smoke reported
   "1@198" for everything). Replaced by per-datagram stamps in `D`.
3. **P6's unit was wrong** (whole skb instead of payload) and the correcting
   arms 1017..1022 were added *after* the first run: they are measurements, not
   predictions, and are labelled so.
4. **I killed a running boot by PID** while adding those arms — the PID was the
   `timeout` wrapper of rep1, not a stray process (the F57 lesson, seventh
   time in the project).
5. **The verifier crashed instead of going red** twice (`slots()` indexing past
   the profile when a mutated `n` exceeded it; `pkt_view()` unpacking three
   values after a five-line rewrite) — both found by the selftest.
6. **The verifier's model was wrong six times**: zero-padded RCV slots, the
   flow label at IPv6 bytes 1..3 (not 3..5), the frame offset 14 (not 16),
   `wany` counting frames on `y2` and packets on `y1`, the stored field vs the
   skb length in the message, and the hop limit of a `skb_segment_list()` tail
   (64, not 63).

## What is not measured, directly

* the mechanism behind the delivery split (queue budget? skb truesize? the
  reader's pace?) — the numbers are in §6 and no claim is built on them;
* **what the extra byte in the byte cap is**;
* the parent taps (§Errors 1): why an `AF_PACKET` socket bound to `y1` in the
  parent netns sees nothing, while the same call in `D` works;
* `CHECKSUM_COMPLETE`, TCP-GRO, tunnels, a real NIC (still none in the project);
* the `l_mid_a` shape instability: in 1 of 4 attempt-pairs the same four
  datagrams became **1 + 2 + 1** packets instead of **3 + 1**, because a poll
  boundary fell between two `sendto()` calls. That is a property of when the
  sender's calls are batched, not of the datagrams — the verifier accepts any
  shape a poll boundary can produce and reports the distribution.

**Not a vulnerability**: all rules were installed by root in the initial user
namespace (F57: the only context where a payload store is accepted at all).
