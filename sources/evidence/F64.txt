The F63 follow-up measured who pays for a store into a GRO aggregate.  This
one measures what the aggregate IS, because the first question turned out to
depend on the second: the same four datagrams give a rule two different
lengths and two different checksum fields depending only on whether the
aggregate is a fraglist or linear.

  nft_payload evaluates on the skb the hook receives.

  With Generic Receive Offload that skb is one packet built out of several
  datagrams, and its UDP header is not any datagram's header:

   512,512,512           fraglist   uh->len 0x0608  uh->check fcc2 = seed(520)
   512,512,512,200       fraglist   uh->len 0x06d0  uh->check fcc2 = seed(520)
   512,512,512,200       linear     uh->len 0x06d0  uh->check 018b = seed(1744)
   64 x 512              fraglist   uh->len 0x8008  uh->check fcc2 = seed(520)
   64 x 512              linear     uh->len 0x8008  uh->check 7ac3 = seed(32776)

`uh->len` is the sum (udp_gro_complete() writes skb->len - nhoff).  The
checksum field is the first datagram's own CHECKSUM_PARTIAL seed on the
fraglist path -- udp_gro_receive() merges without touching the head's field --
and a seed over the whole aggregate on the linear path, where
skb_gro_receive() completes the header.  A rule that reads @th,4,2 sees a
length no datagram in the packet has; a rule that reads @th,6,2 sees a number
that addresses 520 bytes of a 1744-byte packet.

Two consequences that a rule cannot see at all:

1.  The tail.  When the split happens on a device without GSO the trailing
    segment's length is computed, not derived: 208 = 8 + 200 comes from the
    `if (!skb_is_gso(seg))` clause, the only per-segment arithmetic in
    __udp_gso_segment().  The hop limit says which path ran: the fraglist
    split copies only the MAC header into each fragment, so only the first
    segment carries the router's decrement (63,64,64,64) while the linear
    path copies the whole header (63,63,63,63).

2.  Who pays depends on the store's own offset, not on the shape.  Through a
    device without checksum offload a fraglist aggregate ships the senders'
    seeds (fcc3,fcc3,fcc3,fb8b -- auxdata 9, CHECKSUM_PARTIAL, delivered).
    The same aggregate with the same rule, whose store lands four bytes past
    the head instead of inside it, is linearised: skb_pagelen() - 8 no longer
    equals gso_size, the geometry test fails, and every segment is hashed
    (02b0,01b0,00b0,1248 -- auxdata 129).  The rule did not change; the
    offset did.

Two smaller measurements, offered as data rather than as argument:

  *  UDP_GRO_CNT_MAX is 64 and the datagram that did not fit is a plain
     packet, not a one-datagram aggregate: gro_complete() sets gso_size = 0
     when count == 1.  With 65 datagrams one rule instance reads seed(32776)
     for the aggregate and seed(520) for the 65th, in the same arm.
  *  A store into the length field has three fates: rewritten by
     udp_set_len_short() on an aggregate (the store vanishes, 4 datagrams
     delivered), applied verbatim on a lone datagram (the receiver trims to
     32 bytes and delivers 24), and used as the base of every per-segment
     checksum delta on a linear aggregate (nothing delivered, +4 errors).

Measured on 7.3.4-T563, 26 arms x 2 boots x 2 agreeing attempts, one rule per
arm at ip6/forward, three netns; every number verified byte for byte by
verify586.py (1271 checks) against a reimplementation of csum_ipv6_magic()
and of the aggregate's composition.  Not measured: real NICs (every door here
is a veth with bits cleared by hand), BIG TCP (udp_set_len() writes a zero
length at or above GRO_LEGACY_MAX_SIZE -- out of reach at these sizes), TCP
GRO, CHECKSUM_COMPLETE, and the CHECKSUM_NONE provenance, which could not be
made deterministic on this rig and is therefore not used as evidence.


===== SUMMARY.md =====
# F64 — the geometry of an aggregate: the length a rule reads is a sum no
# datagram in it has

Kernel `7.3.4-T563` (`1efbc95771acb424ec876dfa5c32ff1e993f5230b840c27ff00125978cbd2033`),
the same image as F41..F63; nothing rebuilt.
Instrument `vm/differ586_add.c`, mode `geom`, **26 arms × 2 boots × 2 attempts**,
three netns, the F63 topology unchanged: `S` sends, the parent is the router
(`x0` in, `y1`/`y2` out, one rule per arm in `ip6` table `f85p` chain `fwd`),
`D` receives with sockets on 5000 (plain) and 5001 (`UDP_GRO`), a witness chain
in its own netns, and taps on `y1`, `y2`, `y3`, `y4`.

Predicted before the first run: `research/predictions_586.md`
(`7453657736e143a89e34265fa5e8ec3e82ec29b510ac74b8a12c3e507bebcb56`).
This was the debt F63 left: turn 585 froze no predictions at all.

## 1. The rule reads a header that describes no datagram

One socket, one rule, one packet to the rule — and four different numbers,
depending only on how many datagrams the aggregate holds and whether the
aggregate is a fraglist or linear:

| arm | datagrams | `uh->len` the rule reads | `uh->check` the rule reads | NFLOG length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `g_eq_a` (3×512, fraglist) | 3 | `0x0608` = 1544 | `fcc2` = seed(520) | 1584 |
| `g_t3_a` (3×512+200, fraglist) | 4 | `0x06d0` = 1744 | `fcc2` = seed(**520**) | 1784 |
| `g_t3u_a` (same, linear) | 4 | `0x06d0` = 1744 | `018b` = seed(**1744**) | 1784 |
| `g_c65_a` (65×512, fraglist) | 64 | `0x8008` = 32776 | `fcc2` = seed(**520**) | 32816 |
| `g_c65u_a` (same, linear) | 64 | `0x8008` = 32776 | `7ac3` = seed(**32776**) | 32816 |

The length is the **sum** — `udp_gro_complete()` writes `skb->len - nhoff` into
the head's `uh->len` (`net/ipv4/udp_offload.c`), so the rule sees 1744 where no
datagram in the packet is longer than 520. The checksum field is *not* the sum's
seed unless the aggregate is linear: on the fraglist path the head is still the
first datagram and carries that datagram's own `CHECKSUM_PARTIAL` seed. Both
fields are verified against a reimplementation of `csum_ipv6_magic()`
(`fold(pseudo)` for a seed, `~fold(pseudo+packet)` for a device checksum).

## 2. The split: one door carries the aggregate, the other computes each tail

| arm | door | frames on the wire | `uh->len` per frame | hop limit per frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `g_t3_a` | `y1` (GSO_UDP_L4 kept) | **1** × 1798 | 1744 | 63 |
| `g_t3_b` | `y2` (no HW_CSUM, no GSO) | 574, 574, 574, **262** | 520, 520, 520, **208** | 63, 64, 64, 64 |
| `g_t3u_b` | `y2` | 574, 574, 574, **262** | 520, 520, 520, **208** | 63, 63, 63, 63 |

`208 = 8 + 200` is the only segment length the segmenter *computes*: it comes
from the `if (!skb_is_gso(seg))` clause (`net/ipv4/udp_offload.c:592-594`), the
one place where the trailing segment is measured rather than derived from
`gso_size`. The hop limit is the sharper witness of which path ran: the fraglist
split copies only the MAC header into each fragment (`skb_segment_list()`), so
only the first segment carries the router's decrement, while the linear path
copies the whole header and all four show 63.

## 3. A store in the tail reaches it — and changes who pays

`@th,1544,4 = a0a1a2a3` addresses the first four bytes of the fourth datagram.
It lands there through the fraglist (`lgp3 = a0a1a2a3 84858687`, `lgp0..lgp2`
untouched) and the socket receives it (`B = 0,1,2,160`).

What it costs is decided by the shape and by the store's own offset:

| arm | shape | store | checksum fields on `y2` | auxdata | delivered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `g_t3_b` | fraglist | — | `fcc3`, `fcc3`, `fcc3`, `fb8b` (seeds) | 9 (PARTIAL) | 4 |
| `g_t3_len_b` | fraglist | `@th,4,2` in the head | `fcc3`, `fcc3`, `fcc3`, `fb8b` (seeds) | 9 | 4 |
| `g_t3_w_b` | fraglist | `@th,1544,4` in the tail | **`02b0`, `01b0`, `00b0`, `1248`** (real) | **129** | 4 |
| `g_t3u_b` | linear | — | `02b0`, `01b0`, `00b0`, `cf88` (real) | 129 | 4 |
| `g_t3u_w_b` | linear | `@th,1544,4` | `02b0`, `01b0`, `00b0`, `1248` (real) | 129 | 4 |

This refines F63's rule. F63 measured that a fraglist aggregate ships seeds and
nobody pays; that is true of a store **inside the head**. A store that reaches
past the head pulls data out of `frag_list`, the geometry test
(`skb_pagelen(gso_skb) - 8 == gso_size`) fails, the aggregate is linearised, and
then *every* segment is hashed — `1248` is the tail's own checksum, computed
over 208 bytes of the *stored* datagram. Same shape, same length, same rule:
whether the fraglist pays is decided by four bytes of the rule's own offset.

## 4. Two bytes of length, three fates

`@th,4,2 = 0x0020` — the same instruction, three different packets:

| arm | what the rule's packet is | what happened | delivered |
|---|---|---|---|
| `g_t3_len_b` (fraglist) | an aggregate | head length put back to 520 by `udp_set_len_short(udp_hdr(skb), 8+mss)`; the other three fragments were never touched | **4**, `Udp6InErrors` 0 |
| `g_one_len_a/b` (one datagram) | a plain datagram | nothing rewrites it: the receiver trims the skb to 32 bytes and delivers **24** | 1, 0 errors |
| `g_t3u_len_b` (linear) | an aggregate | the per-segment delta is computed against the **stored** number (`check = csum16_add(csum16_sub(uh->check, uh->len), htons(newlen))`) so every field is a sum over the wrong pseudo length: `fbff`, `faff`, `f9ff`, `c8d8` | **0**, `Udp6InErrors` **+4** |

The 24-byte datagram is the cleanest number of the turn: the rule writes two
bytes, the packet keeps them, the application loses 488 bytes, and nothing in
the counters says why.

## 5. The cap is 64, and the packet that did not fit is not an aggregate

`udp_gro_receive_segment()` closes the aggregate at
`NAPI_GRO_CB(p)->count >= UDP_GRO_CNT_MAX` (`net/ipv4/udp_offload.c:694,766`).
65 datagrams → the rule counts **2** packets: 32816 bytes and 560. The socket
receives **65** datagrams of 512, in order (`B = 0,1,2,3,4,5,…,63,64`).

The 65th is a *plain* packet, not a one-datagram aggregate, because
`gro_complete()` sets `gso_size = 0` when `count == 1` (`net/core/gro.c:280`) —
and the wire says so: on the linear arm the aggregate's field is `7ac3` = seed
over 32776 while the lone packet's field is `fcc2` = seed over 520. One rule,
one arm, two different lengths under the same instruction.

## 6. `mss` larger than the payload is not a property of the wire (P8 refuted)

A frame sent with `UDP_SEGMENT 9000` and 1200 bytes of payload is a GSO skb with
`gso_segs = 1`; `__udp_gso_segment()` refuses a GSO frame not longer than its
own mss (`gso_skb->len <= sizeof(*uh) + mss` → `-EINVAL`). Predicted: 0
delivered, `Udp6InErrors` +1. Measured: **delivered, on both doors, with x0's
GRO on and off** (`g_mssbig_a/b/ng_a/ng_b`, all 1200 bytes, 0 errors).

The reason is one line of `gro_complete()`: a packet entering GRO with
`count == 1` leaves with `gso_size = 0`, i.e. **GRO does not only aggregate, it
un-GSOs a single-segment GSO packet**. The segmentation state never reaches the
wire; the receiving device's GRO erases it before the door is asked. The cell is
therefore not measurable on this rig at all — it needs a GSO skb that reaches
`__udp_gso_segment()` *without* crossing a GRO-enabled device.

## 7. What the rule cannot see, and what I could not make deterministic

* The rule sees the shape only in numbers it was not given: `uh->len` (the sum)
  and `uh->check` (the first datagram's seed). It cannot tell an aggregate of
  three from a datagram of 1544 except by the fact that no such datagram exists.
* **A datagram that starts a GRO flow which nothing follows leaves the arm's
  window.** `g_mid_a` (512,512,200,512) is one packet of 1272 — three datagrams
  — and the fourth is in neither the rule's message nor the tap (one frame of
  1286 bytes). Whether it is forwarded later or held by
  `__gro_flush_chain()` (`flush_old && age == jiffies` keeps only same-jiffy
  entries) was not isolated.
* **The CHECKSUM_NONE provenance did not survive as evidence.** With frames the
  sender built and checksummed itself, x0's GRO forwards **1 of 4** frames
  (`g_t3n_*`: rule count 1, 560 bytes, one datagram at the socket), while with
  x0's GRO off all four are forwarded and the receiver's GRO makes `[3][1]`
  (`g_t3nd_a`: 4 packets, then 2 frames at `y3`: 1598 and 262). The same four
  frames gave `[4]`, `[3][1]`, `[1]` and `[3]+[0]` in different builds of the
  same instrument. Recorded in the log, asserted as *observed*, **not used as
  evidence for any claim above**.
* `udp_set_len()` writes `uh->len = 0` for an aggregate at or above
  `GRO_LEGACY_MAX_SIZE` (BIG TCP). Not reachable here: 64 × 512 is 32776 and
  veth's `gro_max_size` was not raised to 65536.

## 8. Instrument defects, all mine

1. **NFLOG offsets** — I read the payload as if it began at the MAC header; for
   a `family ip6` chain it begins at the IPv6 header. The first smoke printed
   `lgudp` from the middle of the source address and `lgp0` from payload byte 14.
2. **The warm-up** — added to stop the first datagram escaping the aggregate, it
   went to port 5000 because the child masked `psel` to one bit, and then, once
   the mask was fixed and the flush timeout raised, it stayed in the GRO list for
   200 ms and was forwarded *through the next arm's rule*: five arms carried an
   extra 1584-byte packet. Removed.
3. **`gro_flush_timeout` was 20 ms** — too short to survive a jiffy boundary;
   `__gro_flush_chain()` keeps an entry across a poll-end flush only if it
   arrived in the same jiffy, so one arm in ~30 lost its first datagram to the
   flush. Raised to 200 ms.
4. **One attempt per arm is not enough.** Every arm now sends its traffic twice
   and reports the second attempt only if the first agrees with it on
   (packets, first length, second length, message count): 26 of 26 agree in all
   four recorded boots.
5. **A 66-datagram arm was removed.** 66 datagrams gave `64+2` in four boots and
   `64+1+1` in two: whether the packet that did not fit is joined by the next one
   is a race, not a rule. The 65-datagram arm is deterministic; the 66th
   datagram's fate is this note.
6. **The settle drain between attempts** was printed as an arm (`i=127`) because
   I forgot the quiet bit; harmless, but it made four arms look unstable until
   I looked.
7. **The `mss > payload` arm cannot work here** and the four arms built to test
   it (with and without x0's GRO) are a measured refutation of my own model,
   not of the kernel.
8. `dmesg` after the run holds no splat: unlike F63, nothing in this turn
   tripped a `DEBUG_NET_WARN_ON_ONCE`.
